# How the operations desk runs today

Notes prepared by Farah Nadeem, Operations Desk Lead, for the incoming review. Written in one sitting and not revised since. Everything here is fictional.

---

## What the desk is

`ops@pentlandinfra.com` is the front door of Pentland Infrastructure Partners. It was set up in 2019 so that vendors and site teams had one address to write to instead of chasing individuals. It has never been formally scoped since.

Things that arrive here: vendor invoices and payment queries, site access and gate pass requests, contractor variations and claims, insurance and certificate matters, customs and logistics paperwork, bank and mandate correspondence, regulatory letters that have been forwarded on from somewhere else, data room and counterparty requests on live transactions, and a steady volume of things that belong to HR, IR or IT and arrive here because people know the address.

Volume runs at roughly **120 messages on a normal weekday**, heavier on Mondays and at month end. My rough estimate is that **about 60% of it is not actionable** — newsletters, automated notices, acknowledgements, marketing. The sample day in this pack is a little lighter than usual on noise, around 53%.

## Who works it

Five coordinators: **Omar Sheikh**, **Ayesha Raza**, **Bilal Qureshi**, **Daniyal Khan**, and me. Nobody is full-time on the desk — it sits on top of everyone's actual job.

Cover is 08:00 to 18:00 Monday to Friday in principle. In practice Omar is usually in first and clears whatever he can before 09:00, Ayesha picks up mid-morning, and the afternoon depends on who is free. Nobody works it at the weekend, though people do read it.

There is no rota. When I am away, the auto-reply names Omar and Ayesha, which is the closest thing we have to an escalation path.

## How work is tracked

There is a shared spreadsheet, `Ops Desk Tracker 2026.xlsx`, on the network drive. Columns are: Date In, From, Subject, Category, Assigned To, Status, Notes, Date Closed.

Three of the five of us maintain it. Omar does not use it. Daniyal uses it when he remembers.

Status values in current use, as far as I can tell from the file: `Open`, `In Progress`, `In progress`, `WIP`, `Pending`, `Pending - external`, `With Finance`, `Done`, `Closed`, `Closed - no action`, and a number of blanks. Nobody agreed these. They accumulated.

**Everything is supposed to be logged within two hours of arrival.** In practice, when it is busy, things get answered and never logged — which means the tracker under-represents what the desk actually does, and we cannot tell the difference between a request that was handled and one that was never seen.

## What goes wrong

**Nothing is owned until somebody replies.** There is no assignment step. An item is "yours" once you have touched it, which means two people occasionally work the same request in parallel, and it means anything nobody feels strongly about sits untouched. A vendor invoice that is nobody's favourite job can sit for a fortnight.

**We cannot answer "what is outstanding?"** If a director asks, somebody scrolls the mailbox. Doing that properly takes about ninety minutes and is usually wrong by the time it is finished, because it relies on whether a message is marked read — and we all read things without dealing with them.

**Replied is treated as resolved.** This is the one that actually costs us. If somebody has responded, the item looks closed in the tracker even where the substantive action has not happened. We approved a contractor variation internally in January and told the tracker it was closed; the contractor was never told, and we paid standing time for nine days.

**Chasing makes it worse.** Requesters who cannot see status chase by email and by phone. Each chase generates a new message on the desk, which has to be triaged like any other. Junaid Aslam is the most frequent chaser and he is not wrong to chase.

**Forwarding loses ownership.** Anything that arrives at the wrong internal inbox gets forwarded, sometimes several times, and by the time it reaches us the original request is at the bottom of four levels of quoting. We have missed at least one regulatory deadline this way. I do not think we know how many.

## Things that have been tried

Two attempts at a tool, both abandoned:

- **2023 — a ticketing system.** Web-based, every email became a ticket, every ticket needed a category and a priority before you could reply. It lasted about six weeks. The desk went round it because replying took four clicks instead of one.
- **2024 — a shared task board.** Cards created manually from emails. It worked for about a month, then drifted out of sync with the mailbox, and once it was wrong nobody trusted it. The board is still there. It has 31 cards on it, all from 2024.

Both died the same way. Omar has strong views about this and you should hear them from him.

## Rules that exist, sort of

Compliance has asked for a few things over the years. They are not enforced by anything except memory:

- Data room access on a live transaction is not granted until the NDA is executed and returned. Elena is firm on this.
- Changes to bank details supplied by email are to be verified by phone against a previously known number before anything is actioned. Nobody checks that this happens.
- Anything from a regulator is meant to be flagged to Compliance the same day.

I would not swear that any of these are followed consistently. The honest position is that they depend on whoever happens to pick the message up.

## What I would want from a new tool

Somewhere to see what is outstanding and who has it, without scrolling. Everything else is negotiable as far as I am concerned — but I am one of five, and the others will tell you different things.
