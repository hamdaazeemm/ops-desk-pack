# Conversation 2 — Ayesha Raza, Operations Coordinator

Joined eighteen months ago. Maintains the tracker spreadsheet more than anyone else. Fictional transcript, lightly edited.

---

**You keep the tracker. How much of your day is that?**

Maybe an hour, spread out. It is not really an hour of work, it is an hour of interruptions. Somebody asks me where something is, I go and look, I find it is not logged, I go and find it in the mailbox, I log it, I answer them.

**Why is it not logged?**

Because whoever handled it did not log it. Omar does not use the tracker at all. Daniyal logs about half. So the tracker is maybe sixty percent of what the desk actually does, and the missing forty percent is skewed — it is all the quick things, because those are the ones people answer without thinking.

**Does that matter, if the quick things are done?**

It matters when a director asks what the desk handled last month, because the answer we can produce makes us look like we do half as much as we do. And it matters because I cannot tell the difference between "this was handled and not logged" and "nobody has looked at this". Those are completely different situations and they look identical to me.

**Walk me through a bad case.**

The Zenith variation in January. Bilal approved it internally, wrote on the thread that he was closing it out, and marked the tracker Done. The contractor was never told. Their plant sat on site for nine days and we paid standing time. Everybody involved did their job — Bilal genuinely had approved it. The status said Done and what it meant was "we have made up our minds".

**So what should Done mean?**

The person who was waiting has been told. That is the only definition that would have caught it. But I know what will happen if you build that — people will click Done at the point they usually do, because that is when they feel finished.

**What about the noise?**

Most of it is obvious. Newsletters, out of office, read receipts. I would let something delete those and I would never look at them.

Where I would be nervous is the short ones. "Thanks, received." Ninety-nine times that is nothing. The hundredth time it is a vendor confirming they got the spares, which closes an item that has been open for three weeks and that somebody is still chasing. If a machine bins that, we do not lose a message, we lose the fact that something finished.

**How would you want to see the desk?**

One list. What is open, who has it, how old it is, and what is about to go wrong. Right now the only way to get that is to scroll, and scrolling tells you what arrived, not what is unresolved.

**Would you want the machine to assign things to people?**

Suggest, yes. Assign, no — not to me and definitely not to Omar. He will not accept work he did not pick up, and if the system hands him something at 08:15 he will ignore it and then the item looks owned when it is not. That is worse than unowned, because at least an unowned item is honestly unowned.

**Anything you would want protected?**

The date something first arrived. At the moment, when a thread gets forwarded around internally, the clock effectively restarts every time — it looks like it came in today. That regulator letter had been in the building for two weeks before it reached us, and it arrived on our desk looking brand new.
