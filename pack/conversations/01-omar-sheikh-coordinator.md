# Conversation 1 — Omar Sheikh, Operations Coordinator

Longest-serving coordinator on the desk. Clears the early-morning queue. Fictional transcript, lightly edited.

---

**Tell me how you actually work the inbox.**

I open it at ten to eight with a coffee and I go top to bottom. Anything I can answer in under two minutes, I answer immediately and I delete it out of my view. Gate pass, delivery slot, someone asking for a form — gone. By nine o'clock I have usually cleared thirty, thirty-five messages.

**And the rest?**

The rest I leave for whoever comes next. If it needs a decision or somebody else's input, it is not mine.

**How do they know you have already dealt with the thirty-five?**

They do not, really. It is marked read. That is the signal.

**Farah mentioned two tools that were tried.**

Both of them made my morning slower, so both of them died. The ticket system — every email became a ticket and you could not reply until you had picked a category and a priority from a dropdown. Four clicks before I could type a sentence. For a gate pass. I did the maths once: at thirty-five messages that is well over a hundred extra clicks before I have done any work.

The board was worse in a different way. You had to make a card by hand, so the card and the email said different things within a week, and then everyone stopped believing the board. A tracker that is wrong is more dangerous than no tracker, because people stop checking the mailbox and rely on it.

**What would you accept?**

Something that does not stand between me and the reply box. If it wants to sort things into piles before I get there, fine, I do not care how it decides. If it wants to fill in the tracker behind me so I never open the spreadsheet again, wonderful. The moment it asks me to confirm something before I can answer a two-minute email, I am back in Outlook and you have lost me by Wednesday.

**What if it only asked on the complicated ones?**

Then who decides which ones are complicated? If it is the machine, it will get it wrong sometimes and I will be doing the extra steps on a gate pass anyway. Once that has happened to me three or four times I stop trusting the distinction.

**Is there anything you actually want?**

Honestly? Not much. The desk works fine for me. I am not the one complaining.

If I am being fair — the thing that bothers me is coming in on Monday and finding that something I answered on Friday has been answered again by Ayesha over the weekend, differently. That looks bad to the vendor. So if it could show me that somebody else is already in a thread, before I type, that would be worth something. But that is a tiny light somewhere on the screen, not a workflow.

**And the things that sit for two weeks?**

Those are not really my problem. I am not the person who decides whether we pay an invoice. I move what I can move.

**Last thing — if the desk lead told you to use it, would you?**

I would use it for a fortnight. Then there would be a busy morning, and I would do what I have always done, and nobody would notice until something went wrong. That is what happened both times. I am not being difficult. I am telling you what will happen.
